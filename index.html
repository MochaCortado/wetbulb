    First aid

    Heat stroke: emergency

    Confusion, slurred speech, collapse or seizures, very hot skin. They may still be sweating.

    1. Call emergency services.
    2. Cool them now, before transport: a cold water or ice bath if possible. Otherwise soak skin and clothes with cool water and fan them; ice or cold wet cloths on neck, armpits and groin.
    3. Stay with them until help arrives.

    Heat exhaustion

    Headache, nausea, dizziness, weakness, heavy sweating, thirst.

    1. Move to a cool place; remove extra clothing, shoes and socks.
    2. Frequent sips of cool water; cold water on head, face and neck.
    3. Get medical care. If they become confused or worse, treat as heat stroke.

    Heat cramps

    Muscle cramps or spasms.

    1. Water with a snack, or a sports drink. No salt tablets.
    2. Medical help if cramps last over an hour, or with heart problems or a low-salt diet.

    Getting used to heat

    New workers: at most 20% of a normal day in the heat on day 1, then at most 20% more each day. Returning workers: 50%, 60%, 80%, then 100%.

    After 2 hours of sweating, drink sports drinks or other drinks with salts, and eat regular meals.

    Method and sources
    • Heat stress is WBGT (wet bulb globe temperature), estimated from the hourly forecast with the Liljegren model (Argonne National Laboratory, 2008). Model error is about 1 °C; forecast error adds to it. Forecast wind below 0.62 m/s is raised to 0.62 m/s, as in KNMI's operational WBGT.
    • Work and rest: NIOSH 2016 exposure limits (REL for workers used to heat, RAL for new or higher-risk workers) as hourly averages that include breaks, in 15-minute steps. Clothing adjustments: NIOSH 2016 Table 3-2.
    • Water: US Army TB MED 507 (2022), Table 3-3. Never more than 1.5 qt (1.4 L) an hour or 12 qt (11 L) a day. 1 cup = 240 mL = 8 oz.
    • Outdoor work only. Not for indoor heat, furnaces or other hot equipment.
    • The limits protect most healthy workers, not all. Watch each other; stop at the first signs of heat illness.
